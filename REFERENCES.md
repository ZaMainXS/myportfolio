# Visual references used

The 3D card scenes are built in Three.js (not AI images). They were art-directed against real studio work and product photography:

- Six N. Five — calm sculptural still lifes, believable materials, soft studio light  
  https://sixnfive.com/
- Peter Tarka / Behance — minimal product-object compositions, warm neutrals, controlled accent  
  https://www.behance.net/gallery/139059881/Forms-Shapes
- Roman Bratschi / Behance — abstract sculptural forms, material contrast  
  https://www.behance.net/gallery/71375821/Nonsense-in-3D-N191-200
- Vincent Schwenk — soft minimal 3D objects and material studies  
  https://www.domestika.org/en/courses/3329-playful-3d-design-and-animation-of-fun-objects
- ArtStation HDRI studio lighting references — softbox key / fill, natural contact shadows  
  https://www.artstation.com/marketplace/p/GGkOw/hdri-studio-lighting-016-for-your-3d-rendering
- Real ceramic product photography — matte glaze, edge wear, soft shadows  
  https://modernmetier.com/products/matte-orb-ceramic-wall-light

## Rules applied

- Palette locked to `#F2EFE9`, `#1A1917`, `#6E6A63`, `#D9D4CA`, `#B4532A`, plus natural ceramic/concrete/paper tones.
- One or two objects per scene, off-centre, generous negative space.
- Beveled edges, procedural dust/scratches/roughness variation; no glossy chrome, no glow, no neon.
- Soft key + fill, PCF soft shadows, contact-shadow discs; nothing floats.
- 30° perspective camera (≈50–85mm feel), slight high angle, no heavy DOF.
- Slow pointer-follow only; no idle looping, no particles, no spinning.
- Static SVG fallback if WebGL is unavailable.
